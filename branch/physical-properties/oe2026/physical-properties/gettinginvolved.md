## Maintenance Policy

Our maintenance policy focuses on **long-term availability, minimal change, readability, and reusability**. We intend to preserve the ontology's name, stable IRIs, existing conceptual structure, and CUT-focused reasoning rules so that future users can continue to reference and understand the ontology. Changes will be made only when necessary to correct a documented issue, improve explanations, or accommodate a small number of relevant cases. Each accepted change will be described in the ontology metadata or an accompanying changelog. If a future revision introduces breaking changes, the earlier version will remain available and the incompatible revision will be published under a distinct identifier. We will also preserve matching copies of the ontology, example individuals, terminology list, use case, and queries, together with basic loading instructions.

## Getting Involved

### Clarifying Existing Terminology

The curated terminology list is the primary area for future improvement. Contributors may refine definitions, explanatory or usage notes, examples, and source references to make existing concepts easier to interpret without changing their logical meaning. In particular, the qualitative levels `high`, `medium`, and `low` may benefit from further explanation of their feature-dependent interpretation. These revisions should follow ISO 704-oriented terminology practices and reuse existing concepts wherever possible.

### Adding Limited Corner-Case Individuals

The ontology already includes examples involving a rigid plastic card, rolling pin, sponge, spatula, knife, and scissors. Future additions should therefore be limited to cases not adequately illustrated by the current individuals. For example, a candidate with medium rigidity could help explain a boundary of the existing qualitative model. New individuals should reuse established classes and properties and be accompanied by a short explanation of their purpose, without automatically requiring new reasoning rules.

### Preserving Usability Over Time

Future maintainers should be able to identify the appropriate versions of the ontology and individual data, understand its assumptions and known limitations, and reproduce the documented loading procedure. Routine maintenance will mainly involve keeping files accessible, checking documentation for clarity, and recording necessary updates. Compatibility checks will be performed when substantive changes are introduced, rather than treated as an ongoing evaluation or expansion program.

*Maintenance principles adapted from the [FIBO Ontology Guide](https://github.com/edmcouncil/fibo/blob/master/ONTOLOGY_GUIDE.md).*
